# Logos Atlas

An interactive atlas of Christianity, written from faith and held to the evidence. It covers:

1. **The Atlas.** A pan-and-zoom map from Egypt to China with a time slider running from 2600 BC to today, six layers, and Paul's journeys. About 70 places carry dated events.
2. **Canon and beyond.** Concentric rings from the shared core out to Gnostic and Qumran texts, with the canon line of six traditions (Jewish, Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox, Ethiopian, Church of the East). Also a matrix of the disputed books, how the canon formed, and the strongest case for each canon.
3. **Text and translation.** Lines of transmission for the Hebrew, Septuagint, Latin, Greek New Testament and English streams, plus a Verse Lab comparing 16 key passages: Hebrew or Greek against the KJV, Young's Literal, Douay–Rheims, ASV and Vulgate.
4. **Concordance.** 29 Hebrew and Greek words with Strong's numbers, KJV renderings, and the shift in meaning to modern English.
5. **The Trinity and the Trivium.** Grammar, logic and rhetoric as an analogy of Father, Logos and Spirit, with its sources (Psalm 33:6, Proverbs 24:3–4, Theophilus, Irenaeus, Augustine, Bonaventure, Hugh of St Victor) and its limits.
6. **Ancient traditions.** Egypt, Mesopotamia, Persia, Greece, Rome, Israel, India and China, and eight threads across them.
7. **The Great Conversation.** An influence map from Heraclitus to Tom Holland, and Jordan Peterson's *Maps of Meaning* with a steelman and a Christian critique.
8. **Iconoclasts and orders.** Robert G. Ingersoll in depth, thirteen critics and the answers they drew, and the Templars, Freemasonry and Rosicrucians, with documented history separated from legend.
9. **Evidence**, 10. **Fruits and shadows**, 11. **Then and now**, 12. **The Way**: temptation, virtues, six pillars of shalom, and an evening examen.

A **voice-guided tour** (20 steps) uses the browser's built-in speech synthesis. Where no voice is available, it shows captions and advances at reading pace.

## How claims are marked

Every claim carries one label: **Fact** (documented), **Consensus** (most specialists agree), **Debated**, **Tradition** (handed down, unverified), **Synthesis** (an interpretive reading) or **Faith** (what the Church confesses).

## Run it

Open `index.html` in a browser. It has no build step and no dependencies; the only network request is for Google Fonts.

To produce one self-contained HTML file:

```sh
python3 tools/build_artifact.py dist/logos-atlas.html
```

## Sources and rebuilding the data

Every verse quoted on the site is generated from source texts, not typed by hand. `tools/build_scripture.py` finds every `{{Book.C.V}}` citation in `assets/js/*.js` and writes the exact text to `assets/js/scripture.js`:

- KJV (1769), Young's Literal (1862), Douay–Rheims (Challoner), ASV (1901), Clementine Vulgate: public domain, from [scrollmapper/bible_databases](https://github.com/scrollmapper/bible_databases) (`formats/json`).
- Hebrew: Westminster Leningrad Codex with Strong's lemma tags, from the [Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible](https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb) (CC BY 4.0). Cantillation is removed for legibility.
- Greek: [SBL Greek New Testament](https://github.com/Faithlife/SBLGNT) (CC BY 4.0, Logos Bible Software).

```sh
python3 tools/build_scripture.py <dir with KJV.json … VulgClementine.json, gnt/*.txt, wlc/*.xml>
```

The build fails if any citation cannot be resolved.

The map is generated by `tools/build_geo.py` from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) 1:50m land, lakes and rivers (public domain):

```sh
python3 tools/build_geo.py <dir with ne_50m_land.geojson, ne_50m_lakes.geojson, ne_50m_rivers_lake_centerlines.geojson>
```
